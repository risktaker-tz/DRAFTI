# DRAFTI live-streamer phrasebook

Research compilation for the three live-streamer sound modes (Female / Male /
Funny), 1110 numbered phrases. The modes are additive, situational and
OFF by default; this document is the shared source of truth for what each
mode may say and when.

How it was built: online research across checkers/draughts references,
esports casting guides, streaming-engagement best-practice articles and
checkers community pages (sources below), then organized by on-stream
situation so the app can pick lines that match the real game state.

Ground rules baked into every category:
- Accuracy first: a line is only spoken when the game state truly matches
  (a "blunder" line only after the engine verifies a mistake, never on a
  forced move).
- One CTA at a time; CTAs live at natural breaks (game start, engine
  think, between games), never over an exciting moment.
- Humor targets the streamer's own play, never the user, and no offensive,
  profane or abusive language anywhere.
- The streamer layer is a guest in the existing sound design: with every
  mode OFF, the app behaves exactly as before.

- Wikipedia — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkers (variant rules, flying kings,
  forced capture differences, board sizes)
- The Esports Glossary — British Esports Federation
  (britishesports.org/the-hub/about-esports/the-esports-glossary): caster,
  colour caster, shoutcaster vocabulary
- SteelSeries — "How to Get Started as an Esports Shoutcaster"
  (steelseries.com/blog/how-get-started-esports-shoutcaster-400): hype
  pacing, play-by-play vs colour commentary
- Call-to-action practice for creators: scrollscript.ai (why "like, share,
  comment AND subscribe" stacks fail — one decision beats four),
  revive.social CTA examples, getfutura.com CTA verbs, writerslife.org
  personalized CTAs ("join my journey")
- Nigeria Association of Draught & Checkers (public Facebook videos):
  street-draughts culture, named traps ("classical trap", "mighty trap"),
  board proverbs — inspiration for the culture corner
- Chess-on-Twitch clips (twitch.tv/chess): live blunder reactions, "clip
  it" and chat-culture phrasing adapted to checkers
